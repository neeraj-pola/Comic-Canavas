Identity onboarding

One-time per person, Modal + Leonardo

This is where your face enters the system. Done once when you sign up, again when your look changes, and once per friend you want as a recurring side character. Everything downstream that keeps the character looking like you depends on this step.

In 15–25 selfies: varied angles, lighting, expressions, no sunglasses, plus 3 optional full-body shots for clothing
Out identity LoRA (Flux), Character Reference set (Leonardo), ArcFace identity embedding for the critic, a "look card" (hair, glasses, skin tone, signature outfit)
Pick: a five-stage mini pipeline. (1) Face detection and crop with RetinaFace, reject blur and duplicates. (2) ArcFace embedding averaged over the set, stored for the critic. (3) A VLM writes the look card from the photos so the prompt writer can describe you in words. (4) Flux identity LoRA trained on Modal with ai-toolkit, ~20 min, 800–1200 steps, one trigger token. (5) The 4–6 best crops uploaded to Leonardo as Character Reference images. Side characters go through the same five stages with fewer photos.
OptionVerdictWhy
Flux LoRA (ai-toolkit / kohya) on ModalBestStrongest identity under stylization, open weights, the only path that lets Diffusion-DPO learn your taste later.
Leonardo Character ReferenceBest (no-ops)No training, works immediately, good for the daily production strip. Weaker under heavy stylization and not trainable.
IP-Adapter FaceID / InstantID (SDXL)GoodZero-shot identity from one photo; decent, but older base models and a dated look.
PuLID / InstantID for FluxLaterZero-shot identity on Flux without training. Promising alternative to the LoRA if retraining per look change becomes annoying.
Describe the face in text onlyAvoid"Man with glasses and dark hair" matches millions of people; identity drifts every panel.
Train on someone else's photos or an artist's charactersAvoidConsent and copyright. Friends opt in with their own photos; the style is your own style card, not a named artist.
  • Quality levers: photo variety matters more than count; include 2–3 photos in your usual outfit so clothing becomes part of the identity; caption each training image with the trigger token plus a plain description.
  • Metric: ArcFace similarity between generated panels and the reference embedding, target above 0.45 in the chosen style; time from upload to first usable strip, target under 30 min.

Input: text or audio

Vercel front end

The only thing the user does every day. Three ways in: type a few sentences, upload a voice memo, or hold a button and talk. Audio goes straight to object storage through a presigned URL so the API never touches the bytes.

In keyboard, file, or microphone
Out job record + transcript or audio_url in R2
Pick: MediaRecorder in the browser (webm/opus), 30 s to 3 min cap, direct-to-R2 presigned upload, Server-Sent Events for progress. A push reminder at a fixed evening time is the single most important retention feature.
OptionVerdictWhy
Presigned upload to R2BestNo egress fees, no audio through Railway, scales to zero effort.
Upload through FastAPIAvoidDoubles bandwidth, ties up a worker, adds a failure point.
Telegram or WhatsApp voice notes as inputLaterGreat for habit, the note is already a file. Add once the web flow is stable.
Browser speech-to-text (Web Speech API)AvoidChrome-only, poor on names and jargon, no file to re-transcribe later. Fine for a demo, not for production.

Whisper ASR

Groq API, worker fallback

Turns the voice memo into text with word timestamps. This step is deliberately boring: English ASR is solved, so buy it rather than build it, and spend the engineering on what comes after.

In audio_url, a vocabulary hint list from memory
Out transcript, segments with timestamps, language, confidence
Pick: Groq whisper-large-v3-turbo with a prompt parameter seeded from your memory store (names, tools, places). Fallback to faster-whisper small on the CPU worker if Groq is down. No fine-tuning.
OptionVerdictWhy
Groq whisper-large-v3-turboBestFastest hosted Whisper, a fraction of a cent per minute, same accuracy as large-v3 on English.
OpenAI whisper-1 / gpt-4o-transcribeGoodSlightly better on noisy audio, several times the price. Use if Groq quality disappoints on your voice.
Deepgram Nova-3 / AssemblyAIGoodBest diarization and streaming, but you have one speaker and no streaming need.
faster-whisper on CPU (Railway)FallbackFree, 20–40 s for a 90 s memo on a small box. Keep as a backup, not the default.
Fine-tune Whisper on your voiceAvoidEnglish WER is already low. The accuracy you lose is on names and jargon, which the vocabulary hint fixes for free.
  • Quality lever: the vocabulary prompt. "n8n", "LangGraph", friends' names. Pull the top 40 entities from memory each run.
  • Metric: WER on 20 memos you transcribe by hand once. Expect under 5%.

Beat extractor

LLM, temp 0, structured output

Converts the transcript into 3–6 structured events. No creativity here: only events that were actually said, each with time, place, emotion, people, objects, an importance score, a humor score, and a verbatim quote. This is what memory stores and what the script agent chooses from.

In transcript, date, known people and places
Out BeatSheet JSON validated by Pydantic; flags too_short, sensitive
Pick: Claude Sonnet with structured output, temperature 0, one retry that feeds the validation error back. Keep it out of the RLHF loop; it is an extractor with a hand-labeled test set, not a taste model.
OptionVerdictWhy
Claude Sonnet, structured outputBestLowest hallucination rate on "only what was said" instructions; strong at pronoun and name resolution.
Gemini FlashGoodA tenth of the cost. Switch once you have 50 labeled days and recall holds within 2 points.
Llama 3.3 70B on GroqGoodFast and cheap, slightly more invented detail. Acceptable with a strict schema and the retry.
Fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B (SFT on your labels)LaterWorth it only for cost at scale or offline mode. Needs 300+ labeled days first.
Merge with the script agent into one promptAvoid"Understand the day and make it funny" in one call hallucinates events. Extract cold, then write warm.
Classical NLP (spaCy NER + rules)AvoidCannot infer emotion, importance, or scene boundaries from casual speech.
  • Prompt rules: never infer unstated events; merge sub-events in one scene; copy quotes verbatim or leave null; normalize names against the known list.
  • Metrics: beat recall, hallucination rate (target 0), name normalization accuracy on 30 hand-labeled transcripts.

Script agent

LLM now, DPO-tuned later

Picks the four beats that make the best arc (setup, complication, relief, punchline), writes a caption and optional speech bubble for each in your voice, and sets the emotional tone. This is where the comic becomes funny or warm, so it is the first node that learns from you.

In BeatSheet, mood arc, your last 10 best strips as style examples, humor level
Out Script: 4 panels with scene, action, expression, caption (≤ 60 chars), bubble (≤ 20 chars)
Pick: Claude Sonnet with 10 few-shot examples of strips you rated highly, generating two caption variants per panel so the UI can ask which you prefer. After ~300 caption pairs, replace with Qwen2.5-7B fine-tuned by DPO, served on Modal, and keep Sonnet as the fallback for sensitive days.
Option / algorithmVerdictWhy
Claude Sonnet + few-shot from your own rated stripsBest v1Highest baseline quality; retrieval of your best strips gets 70% of the personalization for free.
DPO on Qwen2.5-7B (TRL, QLoRA)Best v2Offline, stable, works at a few hundred pairs. Direct match for chosen/rejected captions.
ORPOGoodNo reference model, cheaper on Modal. Slightly less stable than DPO at small data; try as an ablation.
KTOGoodUses unpaired thumbs-up/down. Add when you collect more single ratings than pairs.
SimPO / IPOLaterLength-controlled variants. Only if DPO captions drift long.
PPO with a learned reward modelAvoidNeeds four models in memory and online rollouts; with 300 pairs the reward model is exactly what PPO over-optimizes. Keep as a README ablation, not a shipped path.
GPT-4o / Gemini Pro as the baseGoodComparable quality; pick whichever gives you a better humor win-rate on a 20-day blind test.
  • Quality levers: hard character limits on captions (prevents wordy panels), a humor knob that the sensitive flag turns off, and quotes from the transcript reused verbatim so the voice stays yours.
  • Metrics: caption edit distance per day (headline chart, should fall), LLM-judge humor win-rate vs base, panel-count and length constraint compliance.
5

Prompt writer

Small LLM, GRPO-tuned later

Translates each panel's scene into an image prompt for the generator: framing, expression, props, lighting, plus the identity and style tokens that keep the character and look stable. Different generators want different prompt dialects, so this node is generator-aware.

In panel script, generator target (Leonardo Phoenix or Flux), style card, identity reference
Out positive prompt, negative prompt, seed policy, guidance settings
Pick: Gemini Flash or Llama-on-Groq with a per-generator template in v1. Prompts are written in two layers: a short, fixed character clause (simple shapes, thick ink, the look card) and a long environment clause that names 4–6 concrete props, materials, lighting, and a halftone or paper texture, so backgrounds come out rich while the character stays simple and consistent. In v2, Qwen2.5-3B fine-tuned with GRPO where the reward is the critic's identity + style + alignment score. This is your "RL with verifiable rewards" story: no human labels needed, the critic scores every sampled prompt.
Option / algorithmVerdictWhy
Templated prompts + small API LLMBest v1Cheap, predictable, and templates enforce the style tokens every time.
GRPO on Qwen2.5-3B, critic score as rewardBest v2Sample 8 prompts per panel, generate, score, update toward winners. No value network, fits an A10G.
DPO on prompt pairs from A/B picksGoodWorks, but the signal is indirect (you picked an image, not a prompt). Combine with GRPO.
PPO with the same rewardAvoidSame reward, more memory, more instability. GRPO is the right variant here.
Let the script agent write prompts tooAvoidMixes writing voice with image syntax; you cannot tune one without hurting the other.
  • Quality levers: a fixed style card (palette, line weight, camera), face-forward framing for identity, and reward shaping that penalizes prompts over 60 tokens and any text in the image.
  • Watch for reward hacking: the model learns prompts that max the CLIP score with flat, centered, low-detail scenes. Add a diversity term and a small VLM judge on 5% of samples.

Panel generator

Leonardo API or Flux on fal.ai / Modal

Produces three candidate images per panel. The two hard problems are identity (it should look like you every day) and style lock (the strip should look like one artist drew it). Everything else is cost and speed.

In prompt bundle, identity reference (Character Reference image or LoRA id), style card
Out 3 candidate PNGs per panel in R2, generation metadata, cost
Pick: hybrid. Leonardo Phoenix with Character Reference for the daily production strip (best stylized look, no GPU ops). Flux Dev with your identity LoRA on fal.ai as the research track, because it is the only path where Diffusion-DPO can change the generator's weights. A toggle in the feedback UI lets you A/B the two.
OptionVerdictWhy
Leonardo Phoenix / Lucid Origin + Character ReferenceBest lookStrong illustration aesthetics out of the box, built-in character consistency, pay-as-you-go. Closed weights, so no Diffusion-DPO.
Flux Dev + identity LoRA (fal.ai or Modal)Best for learningOpen weights, LoRA in 20 min from 20 selfies, Diffusion-DPO possible. Slightly more generic style without a style LoRA.
Flux SchnellIterationRoughly a tenth of the cost and 4 steps. Use while developing, not for the strip you keep.
SDXL + IP-Adapter FaceIDGoodMature identity tooling, cheaper, but weaker prompt following than Flux and dated aesthetics.
OpenAI gpt-image / Google ImagenLaterExcellent quality but no LoRA or explicit character reference, so identity drifts and nothing is trainable.
MidjourneyAvoidNo official API for this use.
  • Quality levers: a style LoRA (20–40 reference panels) on top of the identity LoRA; fixed seed families per day so panels share lighting; 3 candidates, not 1, so the critic has something to choose from; 28–30 steps on Flux Dev (not Schnell) when detailed backgrounds matter, and a per-place reference image stored in memory so your café or desk looks the same every time it appears.
  • Metrics: identity score distribution, cost per strip, p95 generation time. Target under 25 s for all 12 candidates in parallel.

Consistency critic

CNN + CLIP models on the CPU worker

Scores every candidate on three axes and picks the best one per panel, or sends the panel back to the prompt writer when all three fail. It is the CNN part of the project and the reward signal for GRPO, so its quality caps the quality of everything upstream.

In 3 candidates, identity reference embedding, style reference set, panel text
Out per-candidate scores, chosen index, face box for bubble placement, retry decision
Pick: three small models, exported to ONNX and run on CPU: ArcFace (InsightFace) cosine similarity for identity, DINOv2-small embedding distance to your style reference set for style, SigLIP text-image similarity for caption alignment. Combine with a learned Bradley–Terry head trained on your A/B picks, which is your personal aesthetic reward model.
OptionVerdictWhy
ArcFace / InsightFace for identityBestPurpose-built face embeddings; robust across stylization when the style is not too abstract.
CLIP image-image similarity for identityAvoidMeasures overall semantics (hoodie, glasses) not the face. Passes lookalikes.
DINOv2 for style matchBestSelf-supervised features capture texture and line quality better than CLIP's semantic space.
SigLIP for text-image alignmentBestBetter calibrated than CLIP ViT-B/32 on short captions; still tiny on CPU.
VLM judge (Claude / GPT vision) for all threeTie-breakerMost accurate, but 12 calls per day adds cost and latency and is unusable as a GRPO reward at 8 samples per panel. Use on 5% of panels to audit the CNNs.
LAION aesthetic predictor / PickScore as the starting reward modelWarm startFine-tune from these instead of from scratch; your personal head converges in a few hundred pairs.
  • Reward hacking: identity scores reward face-forward close-ups, so every panel becomes a headshot. Cap the identity weight and add a framing-diversity bonus from the script.
  • Metrics: pairwise accuracy of the reward head on held-out picks (headline number), retry rate, CPU time per panel.

Composer

Python worker

Overlays the caption bar and speech bubble on each chosen panel, assembles the 2×2 strip with a date header, and exports a PNG plus a lightweight JSON layout the web page renders natively.

In 4 chosen panels, script text, face boxes from the critic
Out strip.png, layout.json, per-panel PNGs
Pick: Pillow with your own fonts. Bubble placement uses the critic's face box to avoid covering the character. Render the same layout in the browser from layout.json so text stays crisp and editable.
OptionVerdictWhy
Pillow (PIL) compositingBestDeterministic, fast, no browser dependency on the worker.
HTML template + Playwright screenshotGoodPrettier typography, but adds Chromium to the worker image and 1–2 s per render.
Ask the image model to render captionsAvoidSpelling errors and inconsistent lettering. Text is always composited, never generated.
Saliency-based bubble placement (U²-Net)LaterNicer than face-box avoidance, only worth it once panels get busier.

Comic page and feedback

Vercel front end, FastAPI

Shows today's strip and collects the training data without feeling like a survey. Two panels a day show the chosen image beside its runner-up and ask which you prefer; captions are tap-to-edit; a single thumbs-down regenerates the day.

In strip, candidates, script
Out preference pairs (image), preference pairs (caption), unpaired thumbs, edits, share events
Pick: ask for at most two A/B picks per day, log implicit signals (shared, saved, revisited) as weak positives, and store every event with the model version that produced it so training data is never mixed across checkpoints.
OptionVerdictWhy
Pairwise A/B on a subset of panelsBestCleanest signal for Bradley–Terry and DPO, ten seconds a day.
Five-star ratingsAvoidInconsistent across days; ordinal noise ruins small datasets.
Thumbs on every panelGoodUnpaired but plentiful; feeds KTO.
Free-text critiqueLaterUseful for prompt-writer DPO with an LLM turning critiques into rewrites, but users rarely type.

Memory store

Railway Postgres + pgvector

Beats, people, places, and every panel go in with embeddings. It powers the vocabulary hints for ASR, the known-people list for the extractor, recurring side characters, and natural-language search over your own life ("when did I last see Rahul").

In BeatSheet, chosen panels, people records
Out entity lists, similar past days, search results with panels
Pick: Postgres with pgvector, bge-small-en embeddings computed on CPU, one people table with aliases and an optional reference image so recurring friends become consistent characters.
OptionVerdictWhy
pgvector in the same PostgresBestOne database, one bill, joins between vectors and relational data.
Chroma / FAISSAvoidAnother service to run for a few thousand vectors; no relational joins.
Pinecone / WeaviateAvoidOverkill and a recurring cost for a single user's data.
OpenAI text-embedding-3-smallGoodSlightly better recall, but adds a network call; bge-small on CPU is enough at this scale.

Weekly training

Modal, A10G or A100, cron-triggered

Once a week the scheduler exports the new preference data and runs up to four jobs. Nothing here blocks the daily flow; new checkpoints only go live through the eval gate.

In preference pairs, thumbs, critic logs, current checkpoints
Out candidate checkpoints on Hugging Face Hub, eval report
Pick, in the order to add them: (1) Bradley–Terry reward head on frozen SigLIP + DINOv2 features from your image picks. (2) DPO on the script agent from caption pairs. (3) GRPO on the prompt writer using the critic score as reward. (4) Diffusion-DPO on the Flux identity LoRA from image pairs, monthly once you have 300+ pairs.
AlgorithmVerdictWhy
Bradley–Terry reward modelBestStandard, data-efficient, gives best-of-N gains with zero serving cost.
DPO (text)BestMatches pairwise captions; stable at a few hundred pairs; TRL supports it out of the box.
GRPO (text, verifiable reward)BestUses the critic as a free, dense reward; no value network, so it fits one GPU.
Diffusion-DPOBest (image)Direct preference optimization for diffusion models; the practical successor to DDPO.
DDPO (PPO on denoising steps)AvoidHistoric and expensive; Diffusion-DPO gets the same effect offline.
DRaFT / ReFL (reward gradient through the sampler)LaterPowerful with a differentiable reward, but prone to reward hacking with a small personal reward model.
PPO / RLHF classicAvoidFour models in memory, online rollouts, unstable on small data. Keep one ablation run for the write-up.
Full fine-tune instead of LoRAAvoidCatastrophic forgetting on 300 examples; LoRA with a KL term is what you want.
  • Hygiene: version every checkpoint with the data snapshot that made it; keep a frozen held-out week that no job ever trains on.
  • Cost: roughly 30–45 min on an A10G per week for jobs 1–3, one A100 hour per month for job 4.

Eval gate

GitHub Actions + Modal

A checkpoint only replaces the live one if it wins on the frozen evaluation set and does not raise cost or latency. This is the difference between "I fine-tuned a model" and "I run a learning system".

In candidate checkpoint, frozen held-out week, current champion
Out promote or reject decision, report, automatic rollback on live regression
Pick: four gates. Reward-head pairwise accuracy up by at least 1 point; script-agent win-rate vs champion above 55% by LLM judge; critic mean score not down; cost per strip not up more than 10%. Any failure rejects the checkpoint. Live rollback triggers if the first-candidate pick rate drops for 3 days.
OptionVerdictWhy
Frozen held-out week + champion/challengerBestSimple, honest, and it produces the before/after table recruiters read.
Promote every weekly checkpoint automaticallyAvoidOne bad batch of labels silently degrades the product.
Online A/B between two live checkpointsLaterCorrect in principle, but with one user it takes weeks to reach significance. Use offline gates first.
  • Headline chart for the README: first-candidate pick rate by week, with vertical lines where checkpoints were promoted.
